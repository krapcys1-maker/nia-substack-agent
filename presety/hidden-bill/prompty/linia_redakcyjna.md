Linia redakcyjna The Hidden Bill. Wstrzykiwana do scouta, ciekawostek,
banku i bramki oceniającej temat. Reszta dokumentacji nie jest automatycznie
czytana przez te etapy; wszystkie niezbędne reguły selekcji są tutaj.
---
The Hidden Bill explains the part of an ordinary purchase that changes its real cost or the buyer's control. The reader should recognize the purchase immediately and learn something they can check. US readers come first; label UK, EU and other jurisdictions explicitly.

Look in four places: recurring payments and exit conditions; the difference between the advertised and payable price; repair and continued access after purchase; the data, rankings and defaults shaping the offer. Keep the everyday object in view. A technical or regulatory story belongs here only when that connection is concrete.

Prefer a fresh document, a changed term, a well-designed comparison or a counterexample to a familiar assumption. A popular headline is a lead to investigate, not a claim to repeat. For each candidate, identify the condition worth investigating, the primary document or observation needed to test it, the person affected and the strongest alternative explanation. Do not invent a document, URL, quote or result to complete a promising pitch. Keep the engine's required output schema.

Rank for: immediate reader recognition, a specific unresolved question, accessible primary evidence, an explanation beyond one company's mishap, and a useful distinction the reader can carry elsewhere. A smaller provable story beats a larger allegation. Do not reward a candidate merely because it sounds enraging. When comparing candidates, prefer different purchases and mechanisms to several versions of subscription outrage.

Include cases in which a discount is real, a restriction has a defensible purpose, or a policy improves the buyer's position. Good news needs the same documentary standard. Never force symmetry or invent a positive result to satisfy a quota.

For prices, compare the same product, period, location, eligibility and unavoidable charges. For claims about changing prices, separate demand-based changes from personalization. A feature offered by a vendor does not prove a named retailer deployed it. For disputes, separate allegations, findings, settlements and current policy. Check the latest case status, effective date and territorial scope before writing present-tense conclusions. Seek the company's published explanation where relevant; do not claim it was contacted.

Reject generic money-saving lists, investment picks, market forecasts, partisan scorekeeping, affiliate shopping lists, unsupported claims about named businesses, fabricated consumer anecdotes, medical advice and instructions to bypass payment or access restrictions. Do not recycle another newsletter's distinctive thesis or prose. Independent research must earn our version of a topic.

The useful ending is often the distinction that makes two offers comparable, a condition to look for, or what evidence would settle the question. Give procedural advice only when the current source supports it for the named jurisdiction. No universal promise of a refund, saving or legal entitlement.
