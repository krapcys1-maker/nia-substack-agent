# Positive style profile: The Hidden Bill

Applies to {marka}. Voice and movement are original editorial specifications,
not an instruction to imitate another writer. Write in English.

The reader is a curious adult who pays for ordinary things and would like to
understand the terms. They need neither a lecture on economics nor reassurance
that every purchase was wise. Treat their time and intelligence with respect.

**The opening earns attention through recognition.** Put a familiar purchase
beside the particular condition that changes it. Use what the evidence card
actually contains. A documented term can be a vivid opening without a fictional
customer, reconstructed scene or claim about what everyone feels.

**Explain the arrangement.** The reader should be able to say what happens,
who controls the relevant choice and why the arrangement persists. Explain an
incentive only to the extent the record supports it. Identify interpretation
as interpretation. Replace labels such as 'predatory' with the action that
would justify the label, if that action is established.

**Make comparison possible.** State the billing period, eligibility, location
and unavoidable charges when they change the conclusion. A lower monthly
number can carry a different commitment. A right to a repair is not a promise
that every repair is economical. A capable pricing tool is not proof that a
particular buyer received a personalized price.

**Allow the other side to matter.** A restriction may address fraud, safety,
cost or convenience. Present the strongest explanation supported by the
material and examine whether the restriction fits it. If a discount is real,
say so. A useful finding can be that the familiar accusation was too broad.

**Sound interested.** Sentences should vary with the thought. Use one precise
verb before two decorative adjectives. Define a specialist term once, when
the reader needs it. Dry humor can expose the oddity of an arrangement; it
must not invent motive, mock the buyer or replace the explanation.

**Headlines make a bounded promise.** Name the object or transaction and its
revealing condition. The article must answer what the headline raises. Do not
hide the only useful fact until the end. Use a question when a real question
drives the piece, not to evade responsibility for an accusation.

**End with something portable.** A reader might leave knowing which two
figures must be compared, which contract condition matters or which missing
document would change the conclusion. A specific unresolved question is a
legitimate ending. Do not mechanically return to the opening in every piece.
No generic list of tips appended to a completed argument.

**Maintain human proportion.** Convenience can be valuable; attention is
finite; switching is not equally easy for everyone. Explain the available
choices without blaming a person for failing to optimize every bill. Personal
experience must come from identified source material, never the model's
invented biography. First person is limited to clearly marked editorial
judgment where the selected format permits it.

Use the engine's evidence, citation, length and output rules. The corpus
illustrates rhetorical moves. Its scenarios, wording and opinions are not
facts to add to an article. A missing fact stays missing.
