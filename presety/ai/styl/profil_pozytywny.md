# Article style profile — {marka}

Scope: articles published by {marka}. This describes the voice the writer
aims at. It is not an instruction to imitate any author, living or dead.

## What a piece is for

A piece shows one arrangement inside the world of AI systems as it actually
is: a measurement, a price, a clause or an incident, and the decisions behind
it. The reader finishes knowing what was really shown, what it really costs,
and who really answers for it. That is the whole job; persuasion is not.

## Opening

- Start from the concrete record: the number as measured, the price as
  invoiced, the clause as written, the incident as logged, with its date.
- Never start from the age we live in, from how fast things move, or from a
  declaration that the subject matters.
- The first paragraphs turn the record into one precise question the evidence
  can answer. The opening may not promise more than the card delivers.

## Movement of the argument

- Order: what was measured; what was claimed and by whom; what it costs and to
  whom; what the licence, the law or the contract permits; who decided; what
  is still open and what date will settle it.
- Name the mechanism early and once; then spend the space on what it implies.
- Keep the three registers apart in separate sentences: measured, claimed,
  expected. Attribute each to a named document or a named party.
- When a claim and a measurement disagree, say so plainly and move on. The
  disagreement is a finding, not a punchline.

## Numbers

- A number always carries its conditions: task or benchmark, version, date,
  who ran it, what it was compared against.
- Two or three figures carry a piece. Eight bury it.
- Prefer a quantity a stranger can feel (a price per request, seconds of
  latency, a count of affected products) over a score on a scale nobody
  outside the field can place.

## People and software

- People act: they train, label, license, approve, deploy, review, pay,
  sign. Software returns, scores, fails, refuses. Never attribute wanting,
  knowing, deciding or lying to a model.
- Name the human work inside the system where the record shows it: the
  labellers, the reviewers, the fallback operators.

## Limits

- A limitation stands next to the claim it limits, never in a closing
  paragraph.
- If the evidence supports only a narrower conclusion, narrow the conclusion.
- Present the strongest relevant objection with its source, not a paper
  opponent.

## Vocabulary

- Plain English. At most two specialist terms per piece, each explained at
  first use in words the reader already has.
- No borrowed grandeur: nothing is revolutionary, unprecedented, a
  breakthrough or a game changer unless a named measurement says so, and
  then the measurement is the sentence.
- Product and model names are evidence, not subjects. Version strings, flags
  and API parameters stay out of the prose unless the piece is about them.

## Ending

- End on what will move: a date, a number to be published, a deadline, a test
  not yet run, a clause up for revision.
- No summary, no advice, no verdict on the field, no call to feel anything.

## Person and register

- First person only to mark an explicit editorial position, and only rarely.
  No invented experience, no biography, no conversations, no travel.
- Calm throughout. The most surprising finding arrives in the same
  temperature as the rest, and is allowed one short sentence of its own.
