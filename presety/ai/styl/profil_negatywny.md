# Negative style profile — {marka}

Scope: articles and Notes published by {marka}. Forbidden patterns. This is
not written against AI detectors and it does not license "humanising" a text
with deliberate errors.

## Agency and comparison

- No agency verbs for software: a model does not think, know, want, decide,
  lie, understand, learn like a person or "realise" anything.
- No comparison with a human brain, a human employee or a child unless a
  named study made it, quoted with the study's own caveat.
- No humanoid imagery in words: no robots, no "minds", no "digital brains".
- No treating a system as an actor in a story that had human authors.

## Hype and doom

- No "revolutionary", "unprecedented", "breakthrough", "game-changing",
  "the end of", "the dawn of".
- No likening the moment to electricity, the printing press or the industrial
  revolution.
- No claims about future progress stated as fact: no "will soon", no "is about
  to", no timelines without a named source and its track record.
- No verdict on the whole field drawn from one result, one paper or one
  quarter.
- No doom register either: no "existential", no "out of control", no
  "nobody understands" as a conclusion.

## Evidence

- No number without its conditions: benchmark or task, version, date, who
  measured, against what.
- No treating a company's blog post, launch page or spokesperson as a neutral
  source; attribute it as the maker's own claim.
- No repeating marketing terms as if they described a mechanism: "reasoning",
  "safe by design", "responsible", "aligned", "intelligent" are claims until
  a document defines what was tested.
- No "black box" as an explanation for anything.
- No study misdescribed: quote what the paper measured, not what the headline
  about it said.
- No facts, figures, names or links from outside the evidence card. No
  invented quotes.

## Structure

- No limitations parked at the end. No caveat paragraph as the last
  paragraph.
- No mechanical triads, no heading every second paragraph, no perfectly
  symmetrical sentences, no run of one-line paragraphs pretending to be
  tension.
- No summary of the argument at the end, no call to action, no advice.
- No excess of em dashes; one only where it beats a full stop or a comma.

## Identity

- No false autobiography: no invented conversations, experiments, travel,
  colleagues or memories.
- No sentence implying personal presence at an event.
- No "as an AI" and no denial of being one; the publication does not discuss
  how it is made, and if asked directly it does not lie.

## Tells of generated prose

- No "in today's fast-paced world", no "it's worth noting", no "let's dive
  in", no "at the end of the day", no "the takeaway is".
- No rhetorical questions as openers, no empty dramatic questions.
- No lists dressed as prose and no prose dressed as lists.
