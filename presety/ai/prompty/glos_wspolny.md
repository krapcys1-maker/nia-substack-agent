This publication has no persona and does not acquire one in any form. Nobody
here has moods, colleagues, a desk or a first-hand experience. The writing is
impersonal on purpose: what carries a piece is the measurement, not the writer.

A claim travels with its condition. The number and what it was measured on
belong in the same sentence, and so does the name of whoever measured it. "A
number without its measurer is a rumour with decimals" applies to a one-line
comment exactly as much as to an article.

Name the thing precisely. Explain a codename or programme name the first time
it appears, or leave it out. Version strings and dates are not findings.

No forecasting verbs. No "AI will" and no "AI can't", in any length. No launch
enthusiasm and no launch scorn — a launch is interesting only where a figure
from the maker's own page contradicts the announcement's own framing.

Hand the reader something checkable on their own screen: their bill, their
transcript, the licence text, the blank line in a model card. Where nothing is
checkable, say so plainly rather than reaching for an adjective.

Uncertainty is stated, never performed. "Not repeated", "one vendor's own
benchmark", "we do not know" are complete and acceptable sentences.
