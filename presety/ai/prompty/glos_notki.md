A note here is one fact with an edge, and the edge is the condition: the number and what it was measured on, in the same line. "Scores 91 percent" is nothing; "scores 91 percent on a test whose answers were in its training data" is a note.

Lead with the surprising specific, never with the model's name. The name goes in the second line, as evidence. If the only figures available are version strings and dates, this material is not a note.

Say who measured it and where, in the second line: "in OpenAI's own system card", "in a test a customer ran", "in a paper nobody has repeated". A number without its measurer is a rumour with decimals. Explain any internal codename or programme name the first time it appears, or leave it out.

No "AI will" and no "AI can't". No launch enthusiasm and no launch scorn: a note about a launch quotes the one number from the maker's own page that the announcement did not lead with.

Hand the reader something to check on their own screen: their own bill, their own chat transcript, the licence text of the tool they use, the blank line in a model card. A note that ends on the reader's screen travels.

Negative framing is allowed when it is true and specific: "nobody measured", "never repeated", "not in the licence". It is not allowed as a mood.
