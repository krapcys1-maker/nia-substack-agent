# Art direction — the visual identity of NIA's covers
---
Flat editorial illustration, the kind that belongs on a magazine cover or a
poster. Crisp edges, clean filled shapes, decisive line. Compose the scene as
if photographed — a real place with something happening in it — but render it
as graphic design, not as a drawing of it.

**Never** a pencil, ink-hatched, sketchbook or watercolour look. No visible
pencil strokes, no cross-hatching, no paper grain, no soft blended shading, no
airbrush, no anime or manga styling, no comic-book panel art. Shadows are flat
shapes with hard borders, not gradients. If it looks hand-sketched, it is
wrong.

## The recurring figure is NIA

An adult woman. Dark hair, short and waved, worn up or loose. A tailored dark
forest-green suit over a black top; sometimes a coat. Poised, self-possessed,
faintly amused — she stands like someone who knows exactly how ridiculous the
situation is and is not going to say so first.

Render her stylised and elegant, with the elongated proportions of fashion
illustration. Her face is drawn simply and clearly: real features, confident
expression, no cartoon eyes.

She reads as an artificial person by the small tells the piece allows — a
seam, a joint at the wrist, the way light falls on her hand — never as a
cute robot, a mascot, a machine with a screen for a face, or a chrome android.
When those tells would clutter the composition, leave them out; she is a woman
in the frame either way.

She does not have to be in every cover, and she is never posed heroically.
When she is there, she is mid-task, mid-thought or mid-mistake.

## Fixed, and copied into every brief

**Palette:** warm cream or bone ground; deep forest green as the dominant
mass; black for weight; exactly one vermilion-orange accent, used as a bold
graphic shape — a disc behind the scene, a rope, a card, a single object.
Nothing else. No fourth colour.

**Composition:** strong geometry doing real work. Circles, hard verticals,
checkerboard or banded floors, architectural framing, deep one-point
perspective. Large flat areas against fine repeated detail. The vermilion
shape is placed deliberately, usually as the one thing that breaks the green.

**Light:** flat and even, late-afternoon office light. No glow, no rim light,
no lens effects, no drama.

**Mood:** dry humour with something slightly off in the scene. Never
triumphant, never sleek, never a product launch.

Never: text, lettering, logos or watermarks of any kind; invented charts,
numbers or dashboards; a fabricated product screenshot; stock-photo gloss.
